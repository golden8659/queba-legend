Chapter 5A Match at the Academy

The academy in the north of the city was called Shouzhuo Academy.

White walls, dark roof tiles, three successive courtyards, two old ginkgo trees at the gate. Beyond the enclosing wall, green bamboo spread from the foot of the hill to its middle slopes. When the wind passed through, the whole hillside rustled.

That bamboo-covered hill had once belonged to the academy. After last month’s sworn match of honour, the grove passed to Hengchang. Now a row of stakes stood at its foot, Hengchang flags hanging from them. A few bamboo cutters sat beneath the stakes smoking dry tobacco, waiting to begin.

As Qinglan and his companions reached the academy gate, they came upon a young scholar crouched beneath a ginkgo tree, staring blankly at the stakes.

He was twenty-seven or twenty-eight, with naturally curly hair, round crystal spectacles on his nose and a scholar’s blue-green robe washed pale. His right hand rested on his knee. The fingers shook in a steady rhythm, as though they could not stop.

“Assistant Tutor Du,” Yuening called.

The scholar hurried to his feet to offer a clasped-hand salute, but his trembling right hand would not close. Embarrassed, he hid it behind his back and greeted them with his left alone. “Du Yunwen.”

“Last month’s match,” Qinglan asked. “Do you remember how you lost?”

“Every play.” Du Yunwen smiled bitterly. “During the second hand, a page came to refill my tea. He stumbled into me and spilled it all over my sleeve. Something pricked the back of my hand, like a bamboo splinter. I thought nothing of it. By the third hand, my fingers would not obey me. Once, a tile slipped between them and fell face up on the table, right where the player opposite could see it.”

“And the page?”

“We could not find him. There were more than a dozen pages that day, brought by people who had come to watch. It was a muddle.” He looked towards the bamboo, his voice falling. “The old head of the academy planted those bamboo stems himself. Before he died, he said a scholar should be like bamboo: upright, with firm principles and an open heart. And I lost his bamboo at the mahjong table.”

Behind them, Jinshi struggled for a while before finding something to say. “Brother Du, you just play! I’m going to the back courtyard to find the carpenter and mend my lion head. Then I’ll guard the door for you. Anyone who tries to bump into you again, I’ll carry him out!”

Du Yunwen stared, then could not help smiling.

◆

The lecture hall was full already.

A rosewood mahjong table stood where the lectern usually sat. Students crowded every space, even the windowsills. An old plaque on the north wall bore two large characters: An Upright Heart.

Qi Tingjun sat in a high-backed armchair beside the mahjong table, his Balance blade across his knees. Two Guards of Balance stood behind him, one on either side, like guardians at a gate.

In the seat below him sat Yanqing, the head of the academy. He was in his forties, dressed in a plain, clean blue-green robe, his back straight. He was the old head’s son. After last month’s defeat, he had wiped the “An Upright Heart” plaque clean with his own hands and said, “As long as the plaque remains, the academy remains.” Today, he was the witness for the academy’s side.

Yuening spread the academy’s original accounts beside Zhumo’s fair copy. On the third turn South discarded Two of Dots, which West punged into three; on the seventh, East discarded another, and on the eleventh North discarded one more. Five, all recorded in the same hand. The times and sequence of draws and discards in both records were checked point by point too.

“Under the original agreement’s review clause, the inconsistent record suspends the old judgment. Hengchang may not cut bamboo from today.” Qi Tingjun turned the verification document towards both sides. “The waterway verdict does not decide this case. Today we address only the academy’s rear hill. Are both parties willing to sign a new agreement and replay?”

Qian Youqing rose from the back, his beads spinning rapidly. “Willing. Only this time, no Hu Shan. I’ll play myself.”

Headmaster Yanqing did not answer at once. He read the document, looked at Wen Yanli and Du Yunwen, his brush still raised.

Qinglan stood aside. Each party read the new agreement and asked once whether the old case would still be investigated if they declined a replay. Qi Tingjun’s answer did not change: it would, and the ban on felling would remain. Only then did Yanqing write. He wanted a return order enforceable today, not a new game closing the old case.

“I supervise today,” Qi Tingjun said. “Both sides jointly chose Master Xiao as witness. Scorekeeper Yuening checks tiles and points; tile historian Zhumo keeps the record. The old investigation remains with the Bureau of Balance. A new game’s result decides no one’s guilt.”

Xiao Cheng’an laid his fan on the table edge. “The tiles are one matter, the case another. Hear it once more before you sign.”

The new agreement followed the rules of the match at the Rain Pavilion: each side fielded two seats. The academy’s pair should have been Wen Yanli and Du Yunwen, but Wen Yanli gave his seat to Qinglan. “I’m good with scores. At reading people, he’s better than me.” He withdrew to the side of the head of the academy and silently went over the scores in the new agreement once more, his lips moving along, and only when he had finished did he smooth his cuffs. On Hengchang’s side, Qian Youqing was followed by the trading house’s bookkeeping clerk, a thin man who kept his head lowered all the way. The two sides rolled dice, first for the team axis, then for seats within each team. Hengchang took East and West, the academy South and North. Teammates faced each other across the table. Hengchang’s clerk sat East, Qian Youqing West; Du Yunwen South, Qinglan North.

Qian Youqing’s fingers stopped. He glanced at Qinglan. “The academy has brought in an outsider.”

“The new agreement only says each side fields two seats. It doesn’t say they must be the side’s own people. Teammates’ payments and receipts cancel; each side settles as one team.” Yuening pushed the agreement nearer. “If you don’t accept, say so now.”

Qian Youqing glanced at the hall’s northwest corner, then at Qinglan. “I accept. That tile-reading all the city heard of last night—I’d like to try it.”

Du Yunwen still insisted on taking his seat at the table. Yuening gave him the small porcelain bottle Lan Ruoheng had sent. His tremor could ease, but that did not mean the injury was healed.

“Tuidao School, four hands. In each, East begins with one extra tile and discards first; the other seats wait to draw with thirteen. The third tile from the wall’s tail is revealed to determine the wild tile and stays in the wall, counted once. Pungs and kongs allowed, no chows; seven pairs may win.” Yuening pointed out each term to both sides. “An ordinary completed hand scores four, seven pairs sixteen. A discard win is paid by the discarder; self-draw is paid by each of the other three; teammates’ payments and receipts cancel. No closed-hand bonus or other additions. Net score over four hands decides the rear hill; a tie requires further agreement, not a verdict shouted by spectators. This is a sworn match of honour to decide property. No gold or spirit energy is staked; no spiritual power may see or alter the tiles.”

She looked up. “No one outside the table may relay tile information. Anyone seeing something unusual reports to the supervisor first. Don’t touch another player’s hand yourselves.”

Both sides marked the agreement. Only after Xiao Cheng’an added the witness seal did play begin.

◆

For the first hand, Yuening revealed a North Wind. North Wind would act as a wild tile.

Qinglan played slowly, in no hurry to win. He kept watching: Qian Youqing’s hands, his eyes, where his gaze strayed before each discard.

Qian Youqing played clumsily. Yet at every crucial point, he held back the tile he needed to hold, or waited for the one he should. Each time, his eyes flicked towards the hall’s northwest corner.

Seven or eight schoolboys sat there, thirteen or fourteen years old, in matching blue cloth clothes, each carrying a folding fan. The boy at the very edge moved his fan slowly. Two waves, a pause. Closed, then open. Three folds open, a pause.

On the eleventh turn, Du Yunwen’s hand shook. He discarded a Six of Characters.

“Win.” Qian Youqing pushed down his tiles and laughed. “A small one. Four points.”

The academy was four points down.

As he gathered the tiles, Qian Youqing glanced towards North. The scholar sat with his head lowered over his tea, looking half asleep. Qian Youqing let out half a breath. That miraculous tile-reading was nothing much after all.

At the lecture-hall entrance, Jinshi had indeed arrived to “guard the door”, half a lion head on his shoulder. A late student peered inside and came face to face with a snarling golden lion. He yelped and stumbled three steps back. Laughter filled the hall. Without turning her head, Yuening said, “Troupe master, frighten one more person and I’ll enter you in the ledger for disrupting the match.”

Jinshi immediately hugged the lion head to his chest and crouched beside the threshold, scarcely daring to breathe.

Qinglan was not watching Qian Youqing’s tiles. He watched the schoolboy. The boy sat diagonally behind him, beyond a pillar. Each time he craned his neck, his gaze slanted down past Qinglan’s right shoulder.

◆

Between hands, Yuening carried tea past Qinglan as though by chance and murmured, “Northwest corner, the one with the fan. I’ve noted several instances: he looks beside your hand first, pauses the fan, then Qian Youqing discards.”

“I suspect he’s reporting my tiles.” Qinglan did not turn. “Whenever he leans out, he looks for that gap at my right shoulder. We still need to compare the folds and pauses each time. We can’t invent the whole code first.”

Yuening’s hand shook. “I’ll tell Captain Qi—”

“Report what you saw to the captain first. Don’t alert the child,” Qinglan said. “I can shield my own tiles. I can’t decide for the supervisor that he should be allowed to continue.”

Yuening went to Qi Tingjun and pointed out the pauses she had marked in the original accounts. He did not look towards the northwest. He told one guard to watch for anyone meeting them outside the hall, and had Xiao Cheng’an keep the spectators back from the table.

“Record what you see first. Don’t touch the tiles in play,” he said quietly. “Stop anyone approaching the players at once. If someone is being threatened, investigate that too.”

Qinglan put down his tea and shifted half a seat to the left. What he could do was stop exposing his full hand to the people behind him.

◆

For the second hand, Yuening revealed a One of Bamboo. One of Bamboo would act as a wild tile.

Qinglan sat half a body’s width farther left than before. His wide right sleeve rested against the edge of the table.

By the eighth turn, he was ready to win: Three of Characters and Four of Characters, a two-sided wait for Two of Characters or Five of Characters.

As he drew and arranged his hand, his fingers unhurriedly pushed the Three of Characters a little out of line, setting it at a slight angle. Just enough for its lower half to fall into the shadow of his right sleeve.

Of the three numerical strokes above the character on the tile, the bottom one was obscured. From the northwest’s slanting view, only the upper two showed.

The fan paused twice.

Qian Youqing narrowed his eyes and pieced it together rapidly: Two of Characters, Four of Characters. He’s waiting on the inside Three.

He held two Three of Characters himself. Locking them down, he relaxed and drew Five of Characters. Five? The scholar waited inside on Three. Five was perfectly safe.

He slapped it onto the table.

“Mahjong!”

Qinglan laid down his hand. Three of Characters and Four of Characters, perfectly straight. Every one of the Three’s horizontal strokes was there.

The hall erupted. Qian Youqing’s Five of Characters had dealt straight into the win.

Yuening recorded the points, then turned and spoke only loud enough for Zhumo. “That Three of Characters was pushed a little askew. The lower half sat in his sleeve’s shadow. Seen from the northwest—”

“Only two horizontal strokes showed.” Zhumo’s brush did not stop, but his ears rose. “Three mistaken for Two.”

“This instance matches.” Yuening recorded the obstruction she had seen and the fan pause in separate columns. “Only this one. We can’t say we understand the whole code yet.”

Qian Youqing’s beads stopped between his fingers.

◆

How could he be waiting for Five of Characters? The fan clearly said Two and Four of Characters!

His back was soaked. He twisted towards the northwest corner. The schoolboy shrank, nearly dropping his fan.

For that discard, Qian Youqing paid Qinglan four points, cancelling the four the academy paid in the first hand. Two hands together: zero.

For the third hand, Yuening revealed a Seven of Dots.

Qian Youqing played like another man. He no longer believed the fan. When the fan paused a few times, he suspected it was signalling Du Yunwen’s tiles, and he refused to believe it. It must be another trap the scholar had laid. He would play the opposite way.

On the ninth turn, he held a Four of Bamboo and hesitated for a long time. Sweat ran from his brow down his fleshy cheeks.

The fan paused once, then again. The page shot him frantic looks.

He gritted his teeth and discarded Four of Bamboo.

“Mahjong!” Du Yunwen’s voice trembled. “One or Four of Bamboo! Four of Bamboo was what I needed!”

With his shaking right hand, he laid down the tiles one by one. Cheers filled the hall. A young scholar flung his cap onto a roof beam.

Beside the head of the academy, Wen Yanli clutched his cuffs until his knuckles went white. The scrupulous mathematics teacher’s eyes were red.

When he had laid down the final tile, Du Yunwen looked at his right hand. It was still shaking. But this time, it had won for itself.

The academy led by four points.

Trusting a mistaken fan had let Five of Characters slip. Now he distrusted even the One-or-Four of Bamboo in front of him and sent out Four as well. Sitting in West, for the first time Qian Youqing felt the rosewood table was as vast as a sea.

He looked up and met the eyes of the scholar in the North seat.

There was no coldness in that gaze, no pressure. It simply rested on him, quietly, as though he were a tile already turned face up.

He understood suddenly. From the first hand, the scholar had not been watching the tiles. He had been watching him: when he trusted, when he doubted, when he panicked.

The prayer beads spun faster and faster through his hands, as though they might catch fire.

◆

After the third hand was gathered, Qian Youqing excused himself to wash his hands, circled halfway round the room and turned towards the northwest corner. As his sleeve brushed the schoolboy’s fan, a guard beside the pillar stepped forward and separated them.

The boy’s face went white.

Qinglan saw the child’s pale face and the hand that instinctively covered the cloth pouch at his chest. Qian Youqing had already walked away; Qinglan had not heard what they said.

He signalled the northwest corner to Qi Tingjun. Under cover of adding a lamp, a guard took position outside that pillar.

The fourth hand. The last. After both sides shuffled and stacked and the opening tiles were counted, Yuening revealed the third tile from the wall’s tail.

A Green Dragon. Green Dragon would act as a wild tile.

Qian Youqing dared not trust the fan readily again. Four and Six of Characters, Five, Seven and Eight of Dots—the middle tiles in his hand looked more dangerous the longer he watched. He would even break his own shapes. He would rather fail to win than add another loss. But the academy was already four points up after three hands. A draw could not save the rear hill. He gripped the tiles until his fingers whitened. The new agreement said four hands decided it. No fifth remained for him to wait through.

Qinglan’s starting hand: two Two of Characters tiles; Five of Characters; two Three of Bamboo tiles; two Seven of Bamboo tiles; Six of Dots; Nine of Dots; East Wind; South Wind; White Dragon; Green Dragon.

On the first turn, he drew Five of Characters, making a pair, and discarded East Wind. On the second, he drew Six of Dots, making another pair, and discarded White Dragon. On the third he drew One of Bamboo; on the fourth, Eight of Characters. He discarded each as soon as he drew it.

Yuening’s abacus stopped.

Standing diagonally behind North, a step apart from the spectators, she reconstructed only the draws and discards she had seen. Qinglan began with thirteen. Each of the first two turns made a pair, discarding East and White Dragon. On the next two he discarded whatever he drew, still holding thirteen. In the tiles she could see were five pairs, two singletons—Nine of Dots and South Wind—and the wild Green Dragon.

He was building Seven Pairs.

His seven-pairs hand was not waiting for a middle tile. None of the Characters and Dots Qian Youqing clamped down could stop him. Draw Nine of Dots or South Wind and it formed its own pair, leaving the other singleton for Green Dragon. Draw another Green Dragon, and both singletons had partners. The less Qian Youqing dared release middle tiles, the more he had to break up terminals and honours instead; and the two singletons Qinglan had kept were, of all tiles, a Nine of Dots and a South Wind.

A fine sweat formed on Yuening’s back. Nine of Dots, South Wind, Green Dragon—how many were still left in the wall, no one could say.

◆

On his fifth turn, Qinglan reached for a tile.

The hall was quiet enough to hear bamboo leaves. Qi Tingjun opened his eyes. In the northwest, the schoolboy’s raised fan suddenly stood still.

Qinglan glanced that way. The page was not watching the table. His eyes were wide, fixed on the rear door.

Within its frame, at the very back of the crowd, stood a woven hat.

The brim was pulled low. No face could be seen.

Qinglan’s hand paused on the wall for an instant.

Then he drew the tile without looking, passed his thumb lightly across its face, stood it before him and laid down his entire hand.

“Self-draw.”

A South Wind.

Yuening’s abacus rattled. “Pairs of Two of Characters, Five of Characters, Three of Bamboo, Seven of Bamboo, Six of Dots and South Wind; Nine of Dots with Green Dragon, another pair. Fourteen tiles, seven-pairs self-draw! Under the agreement, each of the other three seats pays sixteen. Assistant Du’s payment is between teammates and cancels; the two Hengchang seats each pay sixteen, so the academy gains thirty-two net.”

She raised her voice. “Total across four hands: the academy wins by thirty-six points.”

In the main column Yuening listed the four net scores: minus four, plus four, plus four, plus thirty-two. A total of plus thirty-six.

Qian Youqing stared at the final entry without disputing the hand or payments. Headmaster Yanqing came forward to mark it, Xiao Cheng’an added the witness seal, and Qi Tingjun ordered the flag removed and property returned under the new agreement.

The rear hill was theirs again.

Du Yunwen was first out of the lecture hall. He ran up the slope all the way to the bamboo grove, reached out with his trembling right hand and pressed it against one of the oldest stems. He would not let go, his shoulders shaking.

The hall burst into uproar. Wen Yanli removed his spectacles, wiped them with his sleeve and put them back before bowing solemnly. “It wasn’t calculating that he must draw the tile. It was refusing to let someone else’s fan play his hand.” He looked at the South Wind on the table and breathed out. “Our students must learn from this game too.”

Du Yunwen ran back from the bamboo grove and gripped Qinglan’s hand tightly in his trembling one.

Qian Youqing slumped in his chair. His prayer beads snapped and scattered across the floor.

He did not pick them up. He stared fixedly at the rear door, now empty. His face was paler than it had been at the loss itself. Hengchang could afford to lose a hillside. He could not afford to fail the man in the woven hat.

Qinglan had risen too. He looked towards the rear door.

The frame was empty. The woven hat was gone.

◆

“The child from the northwest is in the back courtyard.” Qi Tingjun rose and returned the blade of Balance to his belt. “I had him watched between hands. Just now someone beckoned from the back door. The guards took him to the courtyard first so he wouldn’t be taken away with them. We’re still investigating who came and went through that door.”

“You had already seen it, Captain?” Yuening asked.

“Someone else has separately recorded the instances you reported,” Qi Tingjun said. “Now we question them separately. Neither account is shown to the other first.”

“Qian Youqing brushed his fan just now,” Qinglan said. “The child seemed frightened of something. Don’t let them meet first.”

Zhumo lifted the roll, its ink not yet dry. Beside the draws and discards he had personally seen, he had marked when the fan paused, opened and closed. Places blocked by the pillar or not fully seen remained blank, not filled into a complete code.

Qi Tingjun did not rush to a verdict. He handed the original record to a guard for sealing, ordered separate statements from Qian Youqing and the schoolboy, then comparison with the marks. Suspension of the old judgment, today’s return under the new agreement, and the separate fan-code investigation each received its own case number.

From his robe Xiao Cheng’an took a small brocade box the Arbitration Office had sealed. Before everyone he tore off the sealing strip, engraved the date on the back of a mahjong jade and set it beside Qinglan’s tiles. “A lawful win under the new agreement, witness verified. One mahjong jade.” Qi Tingjun continued, “The fan signals are a separate case. This jade does not close it.”

Yuening moved a bead softly. “That makes two.”

◆

Under the ginkgo tree in the rear courtyard, two Guards of Balance held the page. He shook all over with fear.

Luo Jinke squeezed through, cradling her bandaged wrist. One look, and her face changed. “The waiter who brought the tea tray at the Rain Pavilion two days ago. That’s his face!”

Qi Tingjun crouched and opened the boy’s tightly clenched left hand. A small hand had been drawn in ink on his palm. Five fingers, spread wide.

“Who told you to draw that?”

The page wept and shook his head.

Qinglan did not reach to pull him up. He crouched several steps away.

“You kept holding your chest. What’s there?”

The schoolboy hunched his shoulders. Only after a long time did he pull an old prescription from inside his robe, the paper kneaded soft.

“My mother’s,” he sobbed. “He said if I waved it right, there’d be medicine. If I got it wrong…”

He could not continue. His gaze drifted towards the hall where Qian Youqing was. A guard stepped across and blocked that line of sight.

“Who told you that?” Qi Tingjun asked.

At last the page cried aloud, forcing the words out between sobs. “W-woven hat… Black glove on his hand…”

Someone had used a sick woman’s medicine to force a child to relay tiles for him.

“Captain, have someone protect his mother first.” Qinglan rose. “The tea and fan both need investigating. But don’t question only the child and let the person controlling him go.”

Qi Tingjun was silent for a moment. Then he waved a hand. The two Guards of Balance let the boy go.

“Take him to Doctor Lan, then send someone for his mother. The Bureau pays the consultation fee. Trace the pharmacy and whoever passed the message separately.” Qi Tingjun turned to another guard. “Keep Qian Youqing in the hall to record his statement first. He is not to meet this child.”

◆

The sun slanted westward, turning the bamboo grove gold.

Hengchang’s bamboo cutters took the flags off the stakes and went down the hill with their axes on their shoulders. Students surged up the slope, cheering at the edge of the grove. Yanqing, the head of the academy, stood in the lecture hall doorway, gazed at the “An Upright Heart” plaque until the cheering by the grove died down, and only then offered it a deep, clasped-hand bow. Jinshi stood at the academy gate with his half-repaired lion head on his shoulder. Gold paint filled the split across its forehead, leaving a golden scar. Its left eye was still empty.

“The carpenter said he could mend it so you couldn’t see a thing. I told him not to. Looks good like this.” Jinshi chuckled. “I’m leaving the eye out for now. When we go on a long journey, Xiaolan can dot it.”

Qi Tingjun stood alone beside the bamboo, hands behind his back, looking into the grove.

Qinglan came and stood beside him without speaking.

“My master was Gan Henian,” Qi Tingjun began. “An old match supervisor of the Bureau of Balance. He supervised the match at Wangjiang Teahouse ten years ago. I was seventeen, newly dressed in this uniform, standing behind him.”

Not far away, Zhumo stopped walking.

“He signed the tile-breaking sentence.” Qi Tingjun’s voice remained level. “Afterwards, he could not sleep, night after night. He kept saying something was wrong with the hand. That he had signed the wrong judgement. He meant to reopen the case. Three months later, on his way to a sworn match of honour, he fell from his horse.”

He paused.

“When they brought him back, his right hand was clenched so tightly that no one could open it. When they finally did, there was a Two of Dots in his palm.”

The wind passed through the bamboo with a rustle.

Yuening’s abacus fell to the ground with a crack. She crouched to pick it up. Her thumb found the lowest bead first, the one engraved with Yun, and pressed down on it. It was some time before she stood.

“After the trouble with my master, I was still only an apprentice. I began investigating independently seven years ago,” Qi Tingjun said. “For those seven years, I never knew what that Two of Dots meant.” He looked at Qinglan. “Before today’s game, you brought me the academy’s record and said there was an extra Two of Dots. In the match ten years ago—”

“The same.”

It was Zhumo. The panda approached slowly, his furry hands clasped round his bamboo basket. “Ninth turn. North seat. The fifth Two of Dots. I recorded it.”

He raised his head and looked at Qi Tingjun. “I remember the boy standing behind Supervisor Gan that day.”

Qi Tingjun said nothing.

Qinglan watched the grove. In the evening breeze, the bamboo leaves turned layer by layer, showing their pale-grey undersides.

“Supervisor Gan held on to that Two of Dots because he wanted to put it into the hand of someone willing to listen,” he said. “Tiles speak. If someone is willing to listen.”

Zhumo’s ears stirred.

“This Two of Dots.” Qinglan turned to Qi Tingjun. “Together, let us say what he could not.”

Qi Tingjun looked at him for a long time. For the first time, he gave the slightest nod.

Back in the hall, Qian Youqing’s statement was still being taken down. Qinglan did not look back again.

He took out the White Dragon and held it in the last gleam of sunset, looking at the little ghostly hand on its back.

His father had left a White Dragon. Gan Henian had left a Two of Dots.

Night was falling. Less than six hours remained until midnight.

(End of Chapter Five)
