Chapter 12Plucking the Greens

On the day of the lion-dance contest, Jinshi woke before dawn.

He sat on the inn’s threshold, the golden lion head in his arms, polishing it again and again. The golden scar on its brow gleamed. So did the dented brass bell in its left eye.

He was still polishing when Qinglan came out.

“Brother. Couldn’t sleep?”

“I slept.” Jinshi chuckled. “Dreamed of Master. I was dotting the lion’s eyes outside the Rain Pavilion. He stood at the second-floor window, nodding to me.”

He looked down at the lion head. His voice grew quiet. “Only when I woke did I remember. That was you.”

Qinglan sat beside him without speaking.

“Xiaolan,” Jinshi asked suddenly. “If I lose today, do you think Master would laugh at me?”

“No.”

“How do you know?”

“Father said your heart burned too hot.” Qinglan looked into the morning mist. “When someone with a heart like that goes up on the posts, the warmth stays, win or lose. What Father feared most was you winning with a cold heart.”

Jinshi stared for a long time, then scratched the back of his head. “Don’t quite understand that.”

“You will when you’re up there.”

Through the morning mist, gongs and drums already sounded from the docks.

◆

Three people were waiting outside when they left.

Liuxia stood with folded arms, chewing peanuts. “Come to watch your Yunchuan lion lose.” So she said. Yet she held a red silk sash of the kind Pinghe Hall used to honour lion-dance troupes.

Meng Chuan stood behind her, holding Xiaohe’s hand. The girl wore clean clothes now. A little colour had returned to her face. Shyly, she pressed a small cloth parcel into Jinshi’s hand. “Troupe master. I steamed these sticky-rice dumplings this morning… Something to eat before you go.”

Jinshi took them, his eyes warming, and swallowed two at once. “Delicious! With these inside me, I can’t lose!”

Liuxia rolled her eyes. “Who was talking about losing just now?”

On the way to the docks, Changfeng told them about the Huo family.

“The Huo family lion-dance troupe has been in Nanchao for three generations,” he said. “When Old Master Huo was young, he and his lion plucked the greens at the docks ninety-nine times. Never lost once. People here say when a Huo lion goes onto the posts, even the sea wind stops to watch.”

“And now?” Yuening asked.

Changfeng fell silent for a moment.

“He’s ill,” he said. “More than half a year. Can’t get out of bed. The physician says he has a month or two.”

“Huo Xiao is his disciple?”

“He found him,” Changfeng said. “More than twenty years ago, after a great storm, Old Master Huo found a child under a wrecked boat at the docks. Took him home, raised him, taught him the lion dance. These past few years, Huo Xiao has been here every day, plucking the greens. Again and again. Winning again and again.”

The boy looked towards the docks ahead. “Grandpa says he’s winning for his master. Lying in bed, his master hears the gongs and drums from the quay and knows he’s won again.”

Jinshi’s step slowed.

◆

The quay was a sea of people.

The plum-blossom posts stood in two rising rows of eight, with the highest post between their far ends, more than twice a man’s height. A bamboo pole projected from its top, with a leafy green lettuce hanging from it. Beneath the lettuce was a red gift packet.

These were the “greens”. The first lion to reach the highest post and pluck them would win.

A little awning had been erected beside the quay. Beneath it stood a bamboo recliner. An old man lay there under thick quilts, wasted to skin and bone, sparse white hair on his head. Two Huo apprentices attended him, one on either side.

His eyes were half closed. But whenever the gongs and drums sounded, his fingers tapped gently on the quilt.

Boom. Boom. Clang.

The lion-dance rhythm.

Jinshi stood in the crowd and watched him for a long time.

◆

“The lion from out of town. Here at last?”

Huo Xiao emerged from the crowd in his close-fitting blue-green clothes, the blue-green lion on his shoulder. He glanced at the head in Jinshi’s arms, then at the awning. His lips pressed together.

“Simple rules,” he said. “Two rows of posts, one lion on each. Two people per lion, one head and one tail. You may contest position and squeeze each other on the final two posts, but no deliberate injury. First to pluck the greens wins.”

“Two people?” Jinshi blinked and looked behind him.

His troupe had stayed in Yunchuan. Behind him were only Qinglan, Yuening, Zhumo, Lu Zhaoye, Pei Zhiwei and Changfeng.

“I’ll do it.”

Lu Zhaoye dropped silently from the top of an arcade column and grinned at Jinshi. “Tails are a catfolk speciality.”

The whole quay laughed. Huo Xiao’s mouth moved too. This time, it was a real smile.

There was half an incense stick’s burning time before the contest. Jinshi pulled Lu Zhaoye behind a column for a hurried lesson in working the lion’s tail.

“Remember one thing. Whichever way I sink, you brace that way. I lift my head, you crouch. I jump, you jump with me.”

“That all?” Lu Zhaoye raised a dismissive eyebrow. “Catfolk are born knowing how to jump.”

He ducked into the tail. Jinshi raised his head; he crouched. Jinshi sank left; he braced right. Wrong way. They rolled into a heap behind the column. The lion’s tail tangled with Lu Zhaoye’s own, and it took an age to free them.

Yuening covered her face. “We’re finished.”

Zhumo, however, was thoroughly entertained. He took out his worn brush. “I’ll record this too.”

On the third attempt, Lu Zhaoye finally braced the right way. Jinshi wiped away his sweat. “Good! That’s how we jump!”

Lu Zhaoye was about to lift the lion’s tail when Jinshi held him back and pointed through the crowd to the gap between the second post and the fourth.

“We don’t leap this. Take the extra post. Wait till I’m settled, then send me forward.”

“You’d normally go round for a gap this small?”

Jinshi patted the old-cloth bundle at his waist.

He tried the step again. His front foot landed firmly, but the rear lagged. He withdrew it and shook his head. “Less strength to borrow. Today we take the steady way.”

He pressed Lu Zhaoye’s hand back onto the lion’s tail.

“Follow my feet. Don’t race the drum.”

Lu Zhaoye watched him, then repeated the steps he had just memorized on the ground. This time both were half a beat slower, but neither foot fell into empty air.

◆

The gongs and drums began.

The blue-green lion went up first.

It did not jump. It flew. Barely had a foot touched one post before the next was beneath it, a flickering blue-green shadow, swift as a swallow skimming the sea. In a blink, it was on the fifth.

The quay cheered.

The golden lion climbed slowly. When the blue lion had passed the fourth post, the golden lion was still following the tested steps, landing on the extra low one. Lu Zhaoye did not hurry to push. Post by post the lion advanced, each landing firm before the next. Under the head, Jinshi’s legs were iron pillars. Beneath the tail, Lu Zhaoye was light as a feather, bracing wherever Jinshi’s weight sank.

The sixth post. The seventh.

On the seventh, the blue-green lion suddenly hung upside down, its whole body tumbling over, held only by its two hind feet hooked over the top. Its head swung in midair as though teasing the sea wind.

Thunderous cheers burst from the crowd. Beneath the awning, the old man’s fingers tapped faster on the quilt.

The golden lion did not hang upside down. It settled firmly on the seventh post, raised its head, reared on its hind legs and lifted its forepaws into the air. Facing the sea, it opened its jaws wide.

The same movement it had made beneath the stars outside the Rain Pavilion on the night of the lion dance.

The crowd fell quiet for a moment. Then the cheering rose louder still.

The blue-green lion paused on its seventh post. Beneath the head, Huo Xiao glanced at the golden lion.

Then, instead of jumping to the eighth post, the blue-green lion lunged diagonally towards the post beneath its rival. Its head swung past, almost brushing the golden lion’s forepaws.

Crowding the post. When two lions fought for a foothold, the first to lose balance had to retreat one post.

The crowd gasped.

The golden lion did not retreat. Its hind legs were nailed to the top. It sank low, taking the full rush of air from the blue-green lion’s leap. Inside the tail, Lu Zhaoye was almost horizontal, his own tail wound tight round the post.

The blue-green lion twisted in midair and landed back on its own post. Beneath the head, Huo Xiao drew a breath.

“Good!” An old Nanchao man led the applause.

For the golden lion. Not the blue-green one.

Yuening gripped her abacus until her knuckles whitened. “He’s trying to make Jinshi jump first. Wants him to miss his footing when he fights for the post.”

Qinglan watched the blue-green lion’s four feet. “He won’t shift him.”

◆

The eighth post.

The two lions reached their eighth posts almost together. The posts stood close, the lions only an arm’s length apart, head facing head.

Only the last remained.

The tallest post stood between them, its greens swaying in the sea wind.

Huo Xiao’s blue-green lion gathered itself.

Jinshi moved first.

The golden lion rose into the air and crossed the gap in a single bound. Its forepaws settled firmly on the highest post. Its jaws opened. The lettuce was right at its mouth.

All it had to do was lower its head.

◆

Inside the lion head, Jinshi looked through its open mouth and saw the little awning beside the quay.

At some point, the old man had pushed himself half upright in the recliner. The two apprentices supported him on either side. Neck stretched, eyes wide, he stared hard at the highest post.

He was watching for his blue-green lion.

His lips moved. Jinshi could not hear him, but he could read the shape of the words.

Xiao’er. Up.

Jinshi clenched his teeth.

He remembered the Wangjiang Teahouse ten years before. He had danced downstairs all day, the gongs and drums shaking the air, without once looking up. By the time he took off the lion head, his master had already left the teahouse. He had not made it in time to see him off.

The lion’s jaws stopped before the lettuce.

“Lu Zhaoye,” Jinshi said inside the head, so quietly only the man behind him could hear. “Let your foot slip.”

Inside the tail, Lu Zhaoye paused. Then understood.

The golden lion’s hind foot slipped on the post. Its whole body tipped back. The forepaws left the highest post. It swayed once, then landed steadily on the eighth.

At that instant, a blue-green shadow flashed past.

The other lion leapt to the highest post, opened its jaws and seized the greens.

◆

Gongs and drums shook heaven and earth.

The entire quay shouted, “The Huo family! The Huo family!”

Beneath the awning, the old man sank back into his recliner, smiling. Two streams of tears ran down his wrinkles. On the quilt, his hand tapped the lion-dance rhythm, beat after beat.

Boom. Boom. Clang.

The blue-green lion leapt down and landed before the awning. Huo Xiao lifted off its head, knelt beside the recliner and offered the greens to his master. The old man stroked the lettuce until he drifted to sleep. Even then, the corners of his mouth remained lifted.

Jinshi came down from the posts, removed his lion head and wiped the sweat from his brow. He smiled at Lu Zhaoye.

One of Old Master Huo’s apprentices pushed through the crowd and ran to him. “Troupe master from Yunchuan. My master asks you to come over.”

Jinshi paused, then went to the awning with the lion head in his arms. The old man had just woken. His eyes were still half open. A withered hand emerged from the quilt and lightly touched the golden scar on the lion’s brow.

“A scar. Good.” His voice was thin as a thread. “A lion that has lost… knows pain. A lion that knows pain knows when to give way.”

A shock went through Jinshi.

The old man had already closed his eyes again, a smile at his lips.

Lu Zhaoye climbed out of the tail and looked at Jinshi. For once, he had no joke to make.

“Troupe master,” he said. “That was a good slip.”

◆

The crowd gradually dispersed.

Huo Xiao took his master home, then returned to the docks alone. Jinshi was still sitting beneath the plum-blossom posts, the lion head in his arms, watching the sea.

Huo Xiao stopped before him.

“You let me win,” he said. Not a question.

Jinshi did not deny it.

“Why?”

Jinshi hugged the lion head closer to his chest.

“Your master is watching,” he said. “On the day my master’s tiles were broken and he left the tea house, I was downstairs dancing the lion. The gongs and drums were too loud. I didn’t hear what happened upstairs, and I never gave him the chance to see me dance.”

He raised his eyes to Huo Xiao. “You still have time.”

Huo Xiao stood motionless. The sea wind snapped the hem of his clothes.

After a long time, Nanchao’s proudest heir to the lion dance slowly clasped his fists and bowed deeply to Jinshi.

“Brother Jin,” he said. “From now on, Nanchao’s lion owes Yunchuan’s lion a contest.”

“Owes what?” Jinshi grinned, stood and clapped him on the shoulder. “Next time, I won’t give way.”

◆

Qinglan had stood in the crowd throughout the contest.

At the moment the golden lion leapt towards the final post, an elusive fragrance drifted to him. Plum blossom flowering in snow.

“Scholar,” said a lazy voice beside his ear. “Your senior fellow disciple reads people better than you.”

Feiyan. Somehow she had come to stand beside him. Scarlet-clad, yet no one in the crowd seemed to see her.

“Why are you here?”

“To make a bargain.” Feiyan watched the lions on the posts, her voice quiet. “The great tide is tonight. The man in the woven hat is going to sea himself. The old man is aboard his ship. If you try to take him by force, you won’t get past him.”

“So?”

“So I’ll keep the old man safe for you.” She turned and looked Qinglan in the eye. “At daybreak, he’ll be standing on the quay, alive and well.”

“In exchange for what?”

Her gaze slowly lowered to his chest.

“The White Dragon inside your robe,” she said. “I can smell it. It’s a Heavenly Tile.”

Qinglan’s hand flew to his robe.

◆

“What do you want it for?”

“My tail,” Feiyan said. “Ghost Hand has one of my tails. They say if I bring them a Heavenly Tile, they’ll give it back.”

She gave a faint smile. “Scholar. You want Heavenly Tiles for justice. I want one to put myself back together. Neither is shameful.”

“How do you know Ghost Hand will keep its word?”

“I don’t,” Feiyan said. “I’ve been bargaining with them for three hundred years. They’ve never kept their word once.”

Qinglan looked at her. “And you still bargain?”

“At the abandoned dye works, you won the hand, yet you had every account recorded separately, one by one.” She watched Qinglan, the rouge at the corners of her eyes burning red in the sun. “A man like that owns every debt, whoever it’s owed to. I’ll wager on it this once.”

Qinglan did not guarantee the Ghost Hand for her. He asked, “And if the man doesn’t return?”

Her smile tightened a little. “Then put it under my name. This time, I’m the one making you the promise.”

On the posts, the golden lion’s hind foot slipped. The crowd gasped.

Something flickered in Feiyan’s eyes as she watched.

“Before dark,” she said. “The far eastern end of the docks. The third pile. Bring the White Dragon. Before daybreak, I’ll bring the old man back. If you don’t come…” She paused. “After daybreak, there’ll be one more body at the bottom of that water.”

She turned into the crowd. A flicker of scarlet, and she was gone.

◆

Towards evening, no one at the inn spoke.

Changfeng sat on the threshold, arms round his knees, staring motionlessly towards the docks.

“You can’t give it,” Yuening spoke first. “Your father left it. The old clues are in it, and we’ve only just worked out how to use it to seal stakes. Hand it over, and whether you get it back is up to them.”

“Refuse and they’ll use Old Hong against us,” Jinshi said heavily. “But giving it doesn’t guarantee they release him.”

“Can we trust her?” Zhumo hugged his basket, ears drooping. “She sits on the Ghost Hand’s side.”

“She saved Xiaohe.” Lu Zhaoye crouched on a roof beam, tail still. “But she also said what she wanted was her own tail.”

They all looked at Qinglan.

The door curtain lifted. Li Boyuan came in soaking wet, a knotted rope in his hand.

“I went down and measured.” He tossed the rope onto the table. “The great tide reaches its lowest point in the hours after midnight. It’ll scour away more than a man’s height of mud down there. The salvage ship is already moving into position. After three in the morning, the water rises again. No more chance.”

Liuxia and Meng Chuan followed him in. Meng Chuan stood at the door, fists clenched. “If you need hands, Pinghe Hall will provide them.”

“So will the Huo family.”

Huo Xiao had appeared outside, carrying the blue-green lion. The sweat on his forehead had not yet dried. “Brother Jin gave me a step. The Huo family gives him a boatload of men.”

Jinshi’s eyes widened. For a long time, he could not speak.

Qinglan sat at the table. Before him lay the red cloth, spread open. Within it, the White Dragon. A clean, bone-white face. Nothing on it.

He looked at it for a long time.

He remembered the night his father had pressed it into his hand. Xiaolan. The tiles are clean. It is people who are dirty.

He remembered Li Xingzhou saying Father had taught children to count tiles in the little town. Once they learned and grew up, no one could conjure an extra tile before their eyes.

He remembered Changfeng at the scull. “When Grandpa’s drunk, he hugs me and cries.”

The White Dragon had held a fragment from ten years ago. That hand, that faint click—Qinglan had seen them many times.

But did it remember a child sitting on a threshold, waiting for his grandfather to come home?

Outside, the last light sank into the sea. The tide began to rise. Hush—hush—closer with every wave.

Qinglan folded the red cloth carefully round the tile and stood.

“I’ll go.”

No one spoke.

Yuening stood too. Hugging her ledger, she pressed a finger once against its inner pocket. Inside lay the sheet of cotton paper rubbed from the White Dragon’s back. A hand with five spread fingers. Seven points the size of pinpricks.

“I’m coming with you,” she said.

Qinglan looked at her.

“I keep the ink rubbing. You keep the White Dragon.” Yuening raised her chin. “Lose one, and we still have the other. I said that. I have to be there.”

The tide sounded closer still.
