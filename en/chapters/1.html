Chapter 1The Unfinished Hand at the Teahouse

The ninth turn.

More than two hundred people crowded the Rain Pavilion. On the stairs, the embroiderers stood shoulder to shoulder. The youngest buried her face against her sister, only one clenched hand showing.

The Tao side was still three points down. This was the last hand.

Thirteen tiles stood neatly before the scholar in the South seat. His blue robe was washed pale; over it he wore green and white bamboo-patterned silk. Sweat had gathered at his temples. At the wall’s tail, a Red Dragon lay face up, its red paint dazzling in the window light.

In East, the tall, thin man tapped the table edge. His right sleeve fell, covering a little patch of tabletop beside his standing tiles.

The scholar reached for a tile.

“Can it still come?” someone murmured in the crowd.

No one answered.

The tile stopped between the scholar’s fingers. Without looking, he brushed its face with his thumb.

Half a day earlier, he had been a poor scholar passing through Yunchuan, with only a few dozen copper coins left in his purse.

He had sworn never to touch the tiles again.

◆

The rain in Yunchuan stopped before dawn.

Qinglan stood on the stone arch bridge, watching indigo, crimson and pale yellow water press together beneath him. Once the Tao family dye works in the west began work, half the river took on colour.

He settled the red-cloth bundle inside his robe. It held a White Dragon, its bone-white face entirely blank.

The cloth was wrapped thick. Through his clothes he felt its square outline, then drew his hand away. Over the years, he had taken it out only when alone at night to wipe it clean, keeping it as an old possession his father had left him.

Ten years ago, his father had been subjected to the tile-breaking sentence in public. A set of bone tiles was smashed onto the stone paving. Qinglan knelt to gather the fragments until his hands were covered in blood. That night, his father took a White Dragon from close inside his robe and handed it to him.

It too had belonged to the smashed set. When his father picked it up, its face was still whole.

Qinglan settled his old book chest on his back and walked down from the bridge.

◆

The Rain Pavilion stood at the crossroads, a three-storey wooden building with a black sign in gold lettering beside its door: Mahjong Arbitration.

Qinglan sat in a corner of the upstairs storytelling hall, counted out two coppers and ordered a pot of coarse tea.

The storyteller Xiao Cheng’an had a white beard and an old folding fan. When his block struck the desk, the children fell quiet.

“It is told that Lord Jinque held one hundred and thirty-six Heavenly Tiles and cast them into the sky—”

Several small faces tilted up together.

“The Heavenly Tiles scattered among mortals, each with divine power. Some guarded a forest; some sank into deep waters and could not be found for generations. We’ll come to the divine objects later. First, the mortal world: the Lord taught the rulers of the lands to settle disputes by tiles. War hurts the people. When a quarrel arose, they would sit down to resolve it.”

“The loser accepts defeat, the winner keeps faith!” the children shouted over one another.

Smiling, Xiao Cheng’an nodded and told of the Lord’s ascension, of recovered Heavenly Tiles brought into the Hall, and of the Tianyuan Mahjong Assembly, held every twelve years. Its winner could enter the Hall of the Mahjong God and ask Heaven one question before all the world.

He closed his fan and suddenly lowered his voice.

“But there is one law no one may break. Tampering with the tiles betrays the Way. The tiles are broken in public, and the offender is barred from sworn matches of honour for life!”

Qinglan’s hand trembled. Tea splashed onto his blue robe.

His father had never admitted cheating. But the verdict had been signed and sealed, and no table had ever agreed to lay that match out again.

The Hall of the Mahjong God. One question to Heaven.

It would open again next year.

He lowered his head to wipe the tea away. His sleeve was already wet, but he did not stop.

◆

The morning session had barely ended when brown-clad clerks blocked the stairs.

Qian Youqing, manager of Hengchang Trading House, rolled his prayer beads as he approached the platform and slapped a red paper onto the desk.

Sworn match of honour: Hengchang Trading House versus the Tao family dye works, west of the city

Dispute: ownership of the western dye-works waterway

Today, early afternoon. Four hands under Tuidao School rules; a revealed tile determines the wild tile.

“Master Xiao, we ask you to witness this afternoon’s match.”

Xiao Cheng’an did not answer at once. Those already heading downstairs stopped too.

A woman with twin hair buns stepped from the crowd. Tao Lingtao, owner of the Tao dye works, was about thirty and usually greeted everyone with a smile. Now her lips were pale.

“Manager Qian, that waterway has supplied our dye vats for three generations. You could build your wharf east or south of the city. Why cut off our water?”

“Cut off or not, we’ll see at the table.” Qian Youqing slowly passed another bead. “Your embroidery workshop out back depends on the dye works too. More than twenty girls—many taken in from the streets, weren’t they? Once the water’s gone and the workshop disbands, you can hardly keep them all.”

On the stairs, a little embroiderer seized her sister’s sleeve. Her sister held her hand, unable to offer a word of comfort.

A curly-haired young woman stepped out behind Tao Lingtao, indigo still staining her cuff.

“The northern apothecary, the academy’s rear hill—you win every time! Does Hengchang hire immortals or ghosts?”

Qian Youqing’s smile paused, then he stepped aside to reveal the tall, thin man behind him.

The man had deep-set eyes and long, slender fingers. He took a bone tile from his sleeve, flipped and flicked it between his fingers, and stood it steadily on the platform.

Red Dragon.

“Hushan,” Qian Youqing said. “He plays this afternoon.”

Hushan bared his teeth at Tao Lingtao.

Only after studying those hands did Xiao Cheng’an put away his fan. “Since the sworn match of honour has been brought to the Rain Pavilion, I shall witness. Early afternoon, downstairs.”

In the corner, Qinglan did not rise. He watched the Red Dragon on the platform as the tea in his cup slowly cooled.

◆

At noon, he spent three coppers at the noodle stall in the back lane for a bowl of plain noodles.

He had just picked up his chopsticks when a low question came from the woodshed immediately beside the stall.

“Everything ready?”

The voice was hoarse. Qinglan glanced sideways. Through the crack in the door he saw the tall, thin man who had flipped the Red Dragon that morning. Manager Qian had called him Hushan.

“Ready. The thing goes beside the tea tray for the third hand. Once the dye girl’s hand stiffens, she won’t even stack the tiles straight.”

“And the stacks at the end of the wall?”

“Same as usual. You shuffle, you stack. The last seven stacks are yours.”

Qinglan’s chopsticks hung above his bowl.

Seven stacks: fourteen tiles. But the wall’s tail still had to be drawn from by each seat in turn. One player could not simply take them all. He had not heard how they meant to do it, only that Hushan was to tamper during shuffling and stacking.

After a brief silence, the other voice said, “The master says, once we win the waterway, Yunchuan’s sworn matches of honour will all be ours.”

The door opened.

A woven hat hung low over an indistinct face as the man brushed past Qinglan. On the back of his black leather glove, a gold-thread hand spread its five fingers.

Only when the man had gone did Qinglan lower his head.

The noodles had clumped.

Go to the Arbitration Office? Would they believe him just because he said he had heard someone arranging to cheat? Accuse the wrong person, and he too faced a three-year tile-breaking sentence. And if he gave his father’s name—

His grip tightened on the chopsticks.

But he did not have to accuse anyone first. He could go back to the Pavilion, find Mistress Tao, and tell her to watch the tea tray that afternoon, to keep her people away from its edge.

If she asked why, he would have to repeat the words from the woodshed. If those words reached Hushan, he would know someone had heard their plans in the back lane.

Qinglan lowered his head and picked up the cold noodles. Once he finished this bowl, he could leave Yunchuan.

He ate them strand by strand, and did not return to the Rain Pavilion to warn anyone.

◆

Early that afternoon, people packed the ground floor of the Rain Pavilion.

The two sides rolled dice, first for the team axis, then for seats within each team. Hengchang took East and West, Tao South and North. Teammates faced each other across the table.

Hushan sat East; Qian Youqing, West. South was the curly-haired dyer with stained cuffs, Luo Jinke. North was Tao Lingtao.

Beside the table, a girl in pale yellow held a small ebony abacus in her left hand and a brush in her right. Her name was Yuening, scorekeeper for the match. When a waiter asked whether more stools could fit beside the table, a lift of her hand was enough to keep the space behind all four seats clear.

Yuening turned the agreement towards both sides.

“Four hands decide the waterway. In each hand, East begins with one extra tile and discards first. An ordinary win scores one; closed hand and pure suit each score two. If both occur in one hand, use only one rate, without stacking. A discard win is paid by the discarder. For self-draw, each of the other three seats pays the hand’s score; teammates’ payments and receipts cancel. Drawn hands score nothing. If the four hands end level, the waterway stays where it is.”

Qian Youqing marked the agreement first. Tao Lingtao read it and marked it too.

“Players who notice something unusual may stop play and request a check,” Yuening continued. “I check tiles and points, Master Xiao witnesses, and the tile historian keeps the original record. Disputed evidence is sealed and sent to the Arbitration Office. No one is seized and judged guilty beside the table.”

She checked the tile box. “An ordinary mahjong set has one hundred and thirty-six tiles. The third tile from the wall’s tail is revealed to determine the wild tile. It stays in place, is not drawn into a hand, and is counted only once. Pungs and kongs allowed; no chows. Wild tiles may replace missing tiles. No spiritual power may be used in this match to see concealed tiles or alter draws and discards.”

Qinglan stood outside the crowd, his feet still pointing towards the door.

Just one look.

◆

In the first hand, Tao Lingtao had opened a pung, and her remaining tiles included two suits. She won off Hushan’s discard. An ordinary hand, one point. Someone on the stairs exhaled.

Qinglan did not. Hushan’s hesitation before that discard recalled the woodshed at noon. But the tile had been legally played into the river. No one could call it crooked from a glance.

In the second hand, Hushan called three pungs. Nine tiles lay exposed, four remained in his hand. Everyone waited for his win until the drawable wall was exhausted. It never came.

Yuening recorded a draw. The tiles were shuffled and stacked for the third hand. Before the opening deal, her brush paused.

The seventeen stacks in East’s wall were neither short nor over. But several stacks at one end stood a little loose, their outer gaps a fraction wider than those beside them.

She looked up and met Hushan’s eyes.

He smiled at her, showing yellow teeth.

Yuening lowered her head and wrote in the ledger’s margin: “Before the third hand’s deal: slight irregularity at one end of East’s wall.”

Qinglan saw her look up from the wall, then write. He followed her gaze to that stretch. The tiles within remained face down. Nothing could be seen.

Just then, the tea tray arrived at the table.

Luo Jinke reached for it and suddenly hissed.

“What is it?” Tao Lingtao asked.

“A splinter.” She shook her hand. “Nothing to worry about.”

Qinglan’s nails dug into his palm.

Beside the tea tray for the third hand.

Luo Jinke had already drawn her hand back. The warning he could have given at noon now stuck in his throat.

By the middle of the third hand, Tao Lingtao was waiting for Three or Six of Characters. Someone behind her watched without breathing. Several turns passed. Neither came.

Hushan’s turn. He drew, inserted the tile into his hand, and pushed the tiles over.

“Self-draw.”

One, Two, Two, Three, Three, Four, Four, Five, Five, Five, Six, Seven, Eight and Nine of Bamboo. Fourteen tiles, all Bamboo.

Pure suit. Closed hand.

“They don’t call him Adept Hu down in Nanchao for nothing,” a merchant beside the stairs murmured.

Someone stared at the orderly Bamboo tiles, face pale. Qinglan only noted the title. A fine hand in itself was no evidence with which to accuse a man.

Yuening moved the beads. “One of the two rates, two points. Each Tao seat loses two. Over the first three hands, Tao is three points down.”

Every sound in the building faded.

Tao Lingtao looked at Luo Jinke. The indigo stain remained on her cuff, but her other hand stayed clamped around her wrist.

◆

Before the fourth hand, Luo Jinke reached out to shuffle the tiles.

As soon as her hand touched the tiles, it jerked violently. Bone tiles scattered across the floor. The tiny needle mark at her wrist had swollen purple-red. Her five fingers were stiff; she could not even close a fist.

Qian Youqing rose. “A player unable to take her seat forfeits.”

“Illness or injury permits one substitute, once only, if the other side does not object.” Yuening pressed the agreement flat on the table.

“No objection!” Qian Youqing spread his arms. “Ladies and gentlemen, who will play a hand for Tao?”

No one moved.

Tao Lingtao first helped Luo Jinke sit down. When the girl tried to raise her hand, Tao Lingtao held her arm and shook her head.

Several old men who had been judging the play stood just outside the table. She approached and clasped her hands. “I don’t ask you to guarantee Tao a win. Only for someone willing to sit down, so we can finish this hand.”

One old man glanced at Hushan. His lips moved, but his foot drew back.

She waited a moment longer before lowering her clasped hands.

The embroiderers on the stairs watched her. The youngest clung to her sister, no longer asking whether they still had the waterway.

Tao Lingtao looked down, gripped her skirt, and slowly released it. When she turned, her knees met the stone paving.

“Please… those girls in the embroidery workshop have no homes.” Her voice broke. “The workshop is their home.”

Qian Youqing’s beads swung before his chest.

“Mistress Tao, kneeling isn’t the Mahjong Way!”

Qinglan’s heel moved half an inch towards the door.

One more step back and he could leave.

Tao Lingtao’s forehead touched the ground. He could not see her face, but saw his own bloodstained hands at fourteen, gathering the broken tiles at his father’s feet, one by one.

Everyone in the building had stood just like this then too.

That night his father had handed him the White Dragon and said, “Little Lan, the tiles are clean. It’s people who are dirty.”

Qinglan stopped.

◆

“I will.”

Those by the door turned first. A scholar carrying an old book chest set it down, crossed the crowd and came to Tao Lingtao, helping her rise.

“My name is Qinglan. I’m passing through Yunchuan. For this last hand, if Mistress Tao trusts me, I’ll play for Miss Luo.”

“An outsider?” Someone at the back peered at his book chest. “Didn’t he see the girl’s hand?”

Luo Jinke braced herself on the table edge. “Sir, you…”

As she spoke, she looked down at her swollen hand. At last she turned aside and yielded the South chair.

Tao Lingtao steadied herself on Qinglan’s hand before letting go. “If you’re willing to sit, Tao asks you to play. We won’t blame you if you lose.”

Yuening looked towards West.

Qian Youqing sized up Qinglan’s patched cuffs and laughed aloud. “Fine, scholar. Sit.”

The little embroiderer on the stairs edged forward half a step, only to be pulled back by her sister. She moved no more, watching the chair someone had finally agreed to take.

“Wait.” Tao Lingtao stopped Qinglan and beckoned towards the stairs.

A camphorwood chest was soon brought down. Within lay a ceremonial robe in green and white: deep green as pine needles, fresh white as a cocoon, gold-thread bamboo leaves embroidered along the collar. Red cords hung with tassels, and a green jade pendant at the waist.

This was finery commissioned for the son of Master Wan in the east of the city, meant to be sent after the match. But today concerned justice for the dye works, and for now it would serve as Tao’s clothing for the table.

“You wear the clothes of the house you play for.” Tao Lingtao draped it over his blue robe and smoothed the collar. “Go up in patches, and they’ll think less of Tao before you begin.”

Qinglan tied the red cord and settled the red-cloth bundle inside his robe.

For the first time in ten years, he sat at a tile table dressed so neatly.

Hushan did not smile. He watched Qinglan’s hands.

“How many years have you played?”

“I haven’t. Only watched.”

Someone laughed. Tao Lingtao’s hand tightened on the chair back, then finally loosened.

Yuening did not laugh. Once seated, the scholar rested his hands beside the table without hurrying to touch a tile. She lowered her head and entered his name in the substitute column.

◆

The fourth hand began.

Before the shuffle, Yuening checked the tiles collected from the last hand: one hundred and thirty-six. Xiao Cheng’an witnessed beside her.

After shuffling and stacking, East received fourteen, each other seat thirteen. Each count was checked again. The third tile from the wall’s tail was turned up and left in place.

Red Dragon.

“Red Dragon acts as the wild tile.” Yuening recorded it.

Hushan discarded Nine of Dots first from his fourteen, leaving thirteen. Then it was South’s turn.

Qinglan began with One, Two and Four of Characters; Five, Seven and Nine of Bamboo; a pair of Three of Dots and Six of Dots; East Wind, South Wind, White Dragon; and Red Dragon. Thirteen tiles, scattered and full of gaps.

An old man behind him shook his head slightly.

Qinglan’s gaze passed the revealed Red Dragon, followed that stretch of wall and reached the man seated beyond it.

Yuening glanced at him.

On his first turn he drew North Wind, kept it and discarded East. When play came round to East, Hushan also discarded North.

On his second turn, Three of Characters completed One-Two, and he discarded Four. On his third, Six of Bamboo filled Five-Seven, and he discarded South. Qian Youqing later discarded another North Wind.

On his fourth turn, Qinglan drew another North, made a pair and discarded White Dragon.

On the fifth, Seven of Dots came. He sent Nine of Bamboo into the river.

Ready.

His fingers moved away from the pair of North Winds. Two were already in the river: all four had been seen.

He examined his pair of Three of Dots and Six-Seven of Dots, then looked back at the wall.

North, Tao Lingtao, drew and discarded. Eight of Dots landed in the river.

Qinglan’s fingertip moved.

On the stairs, an embroiderer leaned forward with him.

His hand settled back.

Winning on his own teammate’s discard would not reduce what the workshop owed.

Tao Lingtao watched without urging him. Qian Youqing laughed. “Scholar, pass this one and no one’s saving you the next.”

“Spectators, step back,” Yuening said suddenly. “Don’t call tiles for the players.”

She had the waiter push the surrounding crowd a step farther away. On the stairs, the embroiderer’s sister pulled her back too.

Hushan drew one and discarded one. Qinglan’s sixth draw was One of Dots. He discarded it.

Seventh turn, One of Bamboo. Discarded.

Eighth turn, Nine of Characters. Discarded.

Sweat fell from his temple. He did not raise a hand to wipe it.

Qian Youqing and Tao Lingtao drew and discarded in succession. East, Hushan, lifted a tile from the wall. His right wrist drew in towards his chest, then slid back beside his standing tiles.

Beneath his sleeve, a patch of white slipped into his hand.

Then another tile landed in the river.

Qinglan’s eyes stayed on that right hand. Behind the standing tiles, he had not seen the face of the tile slipped in.

Hushan tapped the table edge.

South’s turn.

◆

The ninth turn.

Qinglan drew and brushed the tile with his thumb.

Three of Dots.

“Self-draw.”

He laid all fourteen tiles down. Someone on the stairs had barely begun to cheer when he raised both hands.

“No one move any of the four hands.”

But Hushan’s right hand moved.

He did not gather his whole hand. From its right end he drew out a single tile, pinched it by the edge and slid it across the tabletop towards himself. His sleeve dropped, hiding half of it.

“Master Xiao. His right hand.”

The folding fan barred the table edge.

Hushan’s hand stopped. The tile remained between his fingers, half exposed.

“Put it down,” Xiao Cheng’an said.

Hushan gave a laugh. “The scholar wins and won’t even let us clear our tiles?”

“Which tile are you clearing?” Qinglan asked.

The laugh died.

Yuening pushed aside her abacus and told the waiter to hold back the crowd. “Keep the space behind all four seats clear. No one touches the tiles.”

Hushan’s fingers tightened.

“You’re saying I cheated?”

“You drew a tile just now. Then another patch of white came in beneath your right sleeve. I didn’t see its face.” Qinglan watched his fingers. “Please put that one down.”

“You didn’t see, and you dare—”

“He didn’t see what went in,” Xiao Cheng’an cut across him. “I saw the one you’re taking out now. It came from your hand.”

The fan did not move.

“Put it in this clear space. Leave the rest alone.”

The hand held on. Someone on the stairs caught their breath, and a neighbour pressed them still.

At last a tile fell.

Click.

Red Dragon.

Yuening counted Hushan’s remaining standing tiles first. When she finished, she looked up, then counted from the beginning again.

“Thirteen.”

Hushan’s chin lifted a little.

“With the one you took out: fourteen.”

He froze.

“You’ve already discarded. It isn’t your next draw yet,” Yuening said. “Where did the extra one come from?”

“You missed an entry.”

A voice came from beside the counter. “Which one?”

A black-and-white figure with a bamboo basket squeezed to the table corner and spread a paper roll. The tile historian had been recording since the opening deal. Ink still shone on the last line.

“East began with fourteen and had thirteen after the first discard. No pungs or kongs after that. Each time, draw one, discard one.” He touched the final line. “Your last discard is here. Point out the wrong entry.”

Hushan did not look at the paper.

“You’re all in it together!”

“Then don’t just look at paper,” Qinglan said. “Scorekeeper, check the discard river.”

Yuening compared it entry by entry, Xiao Cheng’an checking beside her. The Red Dragon Hushan had pulled out stayed separate in the clear space.

The first discard was listed on its own. After that, normal draws and discards were equal in number. Every tile in the river matched the original record.

Hushan’s left hand suddenly reached towards the table.

“Don’t touch!”

Xiao Cheng’an’s shout stopped him. The waiter stepped half a pace forward. The hand withdrew.

“Count the whole set,” Qinglan said.

Yuening checked Qinglan’s exposed fourteen first, then gathered the remaining hands, discards and wall separately. The Red Dragon indicator at the tail remained part of the original set, counted once only.

Bone tiles clicked together. The building held no other sound.

Qian Youqing’s beads stopped turning.

“One hundred and thirty-six.” Yuening stopped.

Four Red Dragons lay before her.

In the clear space Xiao Cheng’an guarded, another still lay.

The fifth.

Hushan looked towards the door.

“Something’s wrong with the set. Why blame me?”

“Before the shuffle: one hundred and thirty-six tiles, four Red Dragons, checked by the scorekeeper and witness.” Qinglan held his gaze. “Opening counts were checked. The original record was checked against the river. After you discarded, you held an extra tile. After play was stopped, you pulled this one out yourself and tried to put it in your sleeve.”

Xiao Cheng’an turned the roll towards Hushan.

“Tell us here and now which part is wrong.”

The chair crashed over.

Hushan shoved past the waiter behind him and charged for the door. Qian Youqing covered his head and shrieked, “Nothing to do with me!”

A tea table toppled, broken porcelain scattering across the paving.

“Guard the tiles!” Yuening shouted.

Xiao Cheng’an did not pursue. One hand protected the separate Red Dragon, the other pressed the table edge. Yuening guarded the ledger and the tiles gathered in separate groups while the waiter ran after Hushan.

Qinglan went round the fallen tea table and followed to the doorway.

Hushan had already burst into the crossroads, his footsteps swallowed by the crowd.

But on the other side of the doorframe, someone still stood.

A woven hat hung low. Slowly, the man raised a hand. The gold-thread Ghost Hand on his black leather glove flashed in the sunlight.

Qinglan stopped.

Noon, at the woodshed door. The same hand.

The man in the woven hat nodded to him, pressed something onto the wooden sign beside the door, then turned into the stream of people.

When Qinglan pushed outside, the hat was nowhere to be found.

Beside the words “Mahjong Arbitration,” a Red Dragon was embedded in the wood.

He prised it out. The bone was dark yellow, the red paint old and dim. Beneath the final downward stroke of the character was a chip the size of a grain of rice. Five spread fingers were carved on its back. A slip tucked behind it fell into his palm.

The one set apart on the table was new.

This one at the doorway had aged for years.

Qinglan looked back. The building still clamoured; Xiao Cheng’an still guarded the new Red Dragon. Clutching the slip, he lowered his eyes again to the old tile in his palm.

Why had the man in the woven hat left it for him?

(End of Chapter One)
