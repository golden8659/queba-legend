Chapter 4Captain Qi

Dawn had not fully broken when someone knocked on the Rain Pavilion’s door.

The knocks were not loud, but each came at a measured interval, as though the spaces between them had been marked with a ruler.

A waiter rubbed his eyes and went to open the door. As soon as it swung back, he froze.

A line of men stood in the morning mist, all in close-fitting blue-grey uniforms, blades at their waists, straight as driven nails. Their leader stepped forward, and the waiter saw his clothes clearly: deep blue-green robes embroidered front and back in silver thread with a xiezhi, clad in scales, its single horn raised, catching a cold gleam in the fog. At his waist hung a long, slender, straight blade, its ebony scabbard bound in copper, its hilt wrapped in red cord.

Xiezhi robes. A Balance blade.

The waiter knew that dress. The Bureau of Balance: the Dayu office charged with disputes under the Way of Mahjong and major cases of tile trickery.

The waiter’s knees weakened. He almost fell to them.

“Qi Tingjun, Captain of the Bureau of Balance.” The man’s voice was flat and even. “I am here to investigate a mahjong dispute. Is the scholar who played as a substitute here two days ago still in the building?”

◆

Qinglan had barely slept.

The White Dragon lay beneath his pillow. He tossed and turned; whenever he closed his eyes, he saw the little hand with its five fingers spread on the tile’s back. Had his father seen that mark long before? If so, why had he given him only the White Dragon, without a word? He had only just drifted off towards dawn when Jinshi’s booming voice woke him.

“On what grounds?!”

He pulled on his robe and went downstairs. Jinshi stood across the stairway like a barred door. A plaster still covered last night’s wound at his temple. His dark face had turned purple.

The hall was already full. Xiao Cheng’an, Yuening and Zhumo were there. Tao Lingtao and Luo Jinke had hurried over too; white cloth bound Luo Jinke’s wrist. Several Guards of Balance held the front and rear entrances. In the centre of the hall stood a young man with his hands behind his back.

Twenty-seven or twenty-eight, upright and lean, with features sharp as a drawn blade. His lips were pressed tightly together, as though he had scarcely smiled in his life.

He looked up at Qinglan. In that single glance, Qinglan felt himself examined from head to foot.

“You are Qinglan?”

“Yes.”

“Born in Tiandu. Left home at fourteen. Whereabouts unknown for ten years.” Qi Tingjun drew a sheet from his sleeve without looking at it. “Played as a substitute at the Rain Pavilion the day before yesterday. The Tao side was three points down. You were ready in five turns, declined a discard win, and won by self-draw in nine. Normal scoring would have left your side one point ahead. Then an outside tile was found, and Hengchang forfeited the whole match. A scholar who hadn’t touched the tiles in ten years.”

He put the paper away and turned towards the table. Yuening first showed him the fresh seal applied the night before, then laid out the old strip Lu Zhaoye had cut and the examination record. Qi Tingjun checked both fingerprints, recorded the time of opening, and only then broke the fresh seal.

Only one snowy-white Red Dragon remained inside. The two pieces of the old seal stayed beside the ledger; they had not been laid back over the rim of the box.

“Last night, during the hai watch, someone broke a seal of the Mahjong Arbitration Office and took a piece of evidence.” He raised his eyes. “Who was in the storytelling hall? From the breaking of the seal to the removal of the tile, tell me separately what each person saw.”

◆

“A cat took it!” Yuening blurted out. “Catfolk! Orange-striped cat face! His name was Lu Zhaoye! I made an entry on the spot!”

She opened her ledger and pushed it towards Qi Tingjun.

Qi Tingjun glanced down at the line. It recorded that at a quarter to ten at night, a catfolk had broken the seal without permission and removed one old Red Dragon.

“Did you make this entry at the time?”

“Yes. I saw the seal broken and the old tile taken. Afterwards I called Master Xiao over, and he checked the newer Red Dragon left in the box.” Yuening pushed the inspection entry forward. “I wrote it, but check it all the same.”

Qi Tingjun noted the time on his own sheet and turned to Xiao Cheng’an. The old man said that when Yuening called him over, the seal was already broken and only the newer Red Dragon remained. He had checked the remaining tile himself, but had not witnessed the actual breaking of the seal or removal of the old one.

“You were downstairs last night, troupe master.” Qi Tingjun turned to Jinshi. “Did you see anyone on the roof?”

Jinshi opened his mouth and scratched the back of his head. “I… I was inside the lion. I could only see through its mouth. I was watching the clubs down on the ground…”

A young apprentice on the stairs snorted with laughter, then hastily covered his mouth.

Yuening shot Jinshi a murderous look. He hunched his shoulders, then stiffened again and strode down from the stairs.

“So what?” he roared. “Hengchang’s thugs came to steal the evidence last night, and our troupe risked our lives to stop them! My junior fellow disciple stood up for justice two days ago. He was the one who exposed the cheating! Why is the Bureau of Balance questioning him instead of hunting Hu Shan?”

Qi Tingjun did not even look at him.

“I have hunted Hu Shan for three months. From Nanchao all the way to Yunchuan. In every city, he cheats at a sworn match of honour and wins a piece of land, a shop, half a mountain. Every time, he walks away untouched.” He paused. “Only two days ago was he exposed at the table.”

“Which proves—”

“That someone understands tile trickery better than he does.” Qi Tingjun finally turned. “Do you know that Ghost Hand’s finest never cheat in person? They use proxy players. The proxy takes the seat, cheats and takes the blame. The real Ghost Hand sits watching in the crowd. When the proxy is exposed, he steps forward to be the one who exposes him.”

The hall went deathly quiet.

“The day he exposes the cheat, he earns a Mahjong Jade and the whole city cheers.” Qi Tingjun spoke each word distinctly. “The very next night, evidence from ten years ago disappears under his eyes. Next time, every sworn match of honour in the city will invite him to play.”

He looked at Qinglan. “Tell me. Should I have come to see you?”

◆

Qinglan offered no defence.

“You think I am the man watching from the crowd, Captain?”

“That is one possibility I intend to investigate, not a verdict already given,” Qi Tingjun said. “Someone removed old evidence without permission. I will verify what you say, and I will pursue Hu Shan wherever he has fled.”

Qi Tingjun took a wooden token from inside his robe and set it on the table. Blackened wood. A hand on the front. On the back: Nanchao.

“I have found nine of these tokens in seven cities. Every one came from a place where Ghost Hand had cheated. Last night, my men stopped a carriage outside the city, heading south. Its occupants escaped. They left this tenth token.”

Qinglan reached inside his robe and laid Pei Zhiwei’s token beside it. Side by side, the two matched even in the depth of their incisions.

Qinglan did not let go at once.

“I can hand over the wooden token. Please record its provenance before us: Pei Zhiwei found it in the woodshed where Hu Shan changed clothes before taking his seat, then passed it to me. It is not the token from last night’s carriage.”

Only when Qi Tingjun had the items registered separately did Qinglan release it.

“As for going with you,” Qinglan said, “first I’d like to know whether you’re asking me to testify or taking me in for questioning.”

“What do you want to ask first?”

“First, the rules of the Mahjong Way.”

Yuening bit her lip, came out from behind the counter and raised her chin again. “Article Eleven of the Mahjong Arbitration Code: where cheating is suspected in a sworn match of honour, the scorekeeper, witness and tile historian must jointly attest before a person may be detained for questioning. If one party is missing, no arrest. Please check the original accounts, the testimony and the historian’s record separately. You can’t take someone away on a supposition first.”

Xiao Cheng’an stroked his beard and stood beside her. “I am the witness. In forty years, I have never set my seal where it did not belong.”

Zhumo squeezed through the crowd and presented a neatly copied roll with both hands. “The match the day before yesterday—from the first turn to the last, I recorded every play. Captain, you can check them tile by tile.”

Qi Tingjun read it quickly but carefully. His finger paused at the fifth turn: “North discarded Eight of Dots; South did not claim a win.” At “South self-drew Three of Dots,” it paused again.

Then he rolled it up. “I’ll check this against the scorekeeper’s original accounts and Master Xiao’s observations separately. Being written in a record doesn’t remove that step.”

Zhumo’s ears drooped at once.

“As a chronicler, I dare not record falsely.” His voice trembled a little. “Ten years ago, I found something wrong in the record of Qing Yuanshan’s game, but I didn’t dare come forward to testify… I kept it hidden for ten years.”

◆

Qi Tingjun did not answer.

With his hands behind his back, he paced slowly across the hall, stopped at the square wooden table and scooped a handful of tiles from the tile box beside it. They scattered across the tabletop with a clatter.

“You say you read tiles.” He said, “Try a problem. It isn’t a sworn match of honour, it has no bearing on the waterway, and I won’t use it to convict anyone.”

He selected thirteen tiles and set them face down before him, then chose nine more, turning them over one by one in a row.

“Suppose these are my discards from turns one to nine. I’m ready now. Can you tell me what I’m waiting for?”

North Wind, West Wind, Nine of Characters, One of Dots, Eight of Bamboo, Two of Characters, Five of Dots, Seven of Characters, Six of Characters.

Luo Jinke looked from the tiles to Qi Tingjun. “You can guess that?”

Qinglan stood at the table without reaching for anything.

“These are tiles you selected, not the discard river left by an actual game.”

“And?”

“So for which turn they belong to, I only have your word to start with. Even if you did discard all nine, they don’t determine a unique hand. Clearing winds, breaking a shape—there could be several reasons. We’d have to keep looking to know which was real.”

Qi Tingjun’s gaze settled on his face.

“People at the Rain Pavilion say you know with a glance.”

“They weren’t sitting in my seat,” Qinglan said. “And I’m not sitting inside your hand.”

A short laugh came from the doorway and was quickly stifled. Jinshi covered his mouth, but his shoulders still moved.

Qinglan pointed at that Five of Dots.

“When you set this down, you moved it once. You may have meant to change the order, or it may simply have been crooked. I saw the movement. I can’t add a reason for it.”

Qi Tingjun did not answer.

“If you hold Three-Four-Five of Characters, Five-Six-Seven of Bamboo, three East Winds, a pair of White Dragons, then Four and Six of Dots, you’re waiting for Five of Dots,” Qinglan said. “But replace Six of Dots with Five, and you wait for Three or Six of Dots. These nine tiles outside don’t rule out the second possibility.”

“If you want a definite answer, all I can say is: this isn’t enough.”

The hall fell silent.

Qi Tingjun slowly turned over the thirteen tiles before him.

Three-Four-Five of Characters, Five-Six-Seven of Bamboo, Four and Six of Dots, three East Winds, a pair of White Dragons.

Exactly the first example Qinglan had given.

The old sugar-figure seller had only begun to exclaim at the miracle when Qinglan shook his head.

“Happening to match one possibility doesn’t erase the other.”

“I did add that Five of Dots on the spot,” Qi Tingjun said at last. “Three months ago in Nanchao, someone moved discards in the confusion, changing their original order. Afterwards, several onlookers reconstructed the hand from that river with absolute certainty, yet their accounts disagreed completely. I wanted to see whether you would invent a story for me too.”

He looked at the Five of Dots. “By the time I reached the case, the original tiles had been cleared away. It was too late even to check.”

Slowly, he unfastened the Balance blade from his waist and laid it on the table.

The hand that set down the blade was shaking.

“Ten years ago. That match at Wangjiang Teahouse,” he said. “I was there too.”

Everyone stared.

The scroll in Zhumo’s hands slipped half open with a rustle. He gazed wide-eyed at Qi Tingjun’s face, as though turning the pages of memory. “You were… the one standing behind the match supervisor that day…”

He swallowed the rest.

Qinglan’s fingers tightened suddenly.

There had been hundreds of people in Wangjiang Teahouse that day. He remembered only his father’s straight back, the North-seat player’s hand opening the wall, the broken tiles on the floor and the blood in his own palms. He remembered no boy in xiezhi robes.

“You were there?” His voice was rough. “What did you see?”

Qi Tingjun did not answer. He fastened the blade to his waist again, and his face grew cold once more, as though that crack had never opened.

“You didn’t pass off the half you guessed correctly as knowing everything,” he said. “I’ll remember that. As for how the old tile disappeared, I still have to investigate.”

◆

“There is no solid evidence at present to detain you for questioning. I won’t take you in.” Qi Tingjun turned to Yuening. “Do you know the academy north of the city?”

Yuening stared. “Hu Shan won its rear hill in a sworn match of honour last month.”

“Master Wen and Assistant Du submitted a petition for re-examination. The old match’s original accounts and agreement have arrived. We’ll review them at the academy this afternoon. If a rematch is lawfully opened, both sides must sign a clear new agreement too.” He looked at Qinglan. “They’ve asked for a substitute. Go if you wish. Refusing won’t affect my investigation of the old match.”

“If I win,” Qinglan said, “you tell me what you saw ten years ago.”

Qi Tingjun gave him a look.

“What happened back then isn’t prize money for a winner,” Qi Tingjun said. “After the academy inquiry, I’ll tell you what I can.”

He left with his men.

◆

Jinshi dropped onto a bench. It creaked and nearly came apart.

“These people from the Bureau of Balance,” he said angrily, “are worse to deal with than Hengchang’s thugs!”

“He didn’t turn a guess into a verdict just now,” Qinglan said.

“How do you know?”

“I don’t yet know what sort of man he is.” Qinglan looked outside. “His hand shook when he put down his blade. I want to know why.”

Luo Jinke came over, cradling her bandaged wrist. “Young Master Qing, I’m coming to the academy this afternoon too.”

“Your hand—”

“My hand cannot play. My eyes can still see,” Luo Jinke said. “I want to see for myself how that needle reached me two days ago. Who handed over the tea tray.”

Behind her, Tao Lingtao gave a small nod.

Zhumo pressed a newly copied roll into Qinglan’s hands. “The academy’s match last month. I copied it yesterday during the day, but with last night’s trouble I had no time to go through it with you. This afternoon we still have to check it against the academy’s original accounts.”

Qinglan read down the lines. Halfway through, his brow furrowed.

“Yuening. Look.”

She leaned close and slid a finger down the record. Her face changed. “Third turn. South discards Two of Dots. West calls pung, exposing three. Seventh turn, East discards another. Eleventh turn, North discards one more… Five Two of Dots tiles. Again.”

Exactly like the hand ten years ago.

◆

They were about to leave when the door curtain lifted and a woman entered.

She was in her early thirties, wearing a deep-blue gown, a black side braid falling over her shoulder. In her hand was an elmwood medicine chest. She looked at no one’s face when she entered. Her eyes went straight to the white cloth around Luo Jinke’s wrist.

“Lan Ruoheng. I practise at the apothecary in the east of the city. Proprietor Tao sent for me last night. She said a dyer had been pricked with a needle, and the swelling had not gone down in two days.”

She unwrapped the cloth. Luo Jinke’s wrist was swollen like a steamed bun; the blue-purple ring around the puncture had spread to an area half the size of a palm. Lan Ruoheng touched a silver needle to the skin beside the wound and lifted a drop of dark-red blood. She held it near her nose and smelled it.

“The needle was coated with juice of bone-numbing herb. Not fatal, but once it touches you, your fingers stiffen for three to five days and you can’t hold things steadily. Delay treatment, and a lingering tremor can last over a month.” She sprinkled powder on the wound. “Every apothecary keeps this herb in stock. Pounded and spread on a wound, it eases pain. You only ever use a thin layer at a time. But the batches I’ve seen that were boiled down this strong all came through Nanchao’s sea-goods shops. To trace it, we’ll have to check the shops’ incoming and outgoing accounts.”

Nanchao again.

Qinglan and Yuening exchanged a look.

Lan Ruoheng dressed the wound, wrote a prescription and told Luo Jinke to stay away from the dye vats for seven days. As she left, she turned back. “I heard that after the academy lost its rear hill, Assistant Du fell very ill. His hands shook too much even to hold a brush. I thought then it was distress. Now that we have this poisoned needle to follow, I need to examine him again. Shaking hands alone aren’t enough to say the two cases are the same.”

◆

On the way to the academy, Jinshi led the procession, the cracked lion head on his shoulder. He said he would find the academy’s carpenter and have it repaired. Zhumo walked along leafing through the old record, murmuring to himself.

Yuening walked beside Qinglan with the ledger in her arms. After a while, she spoke suddenly.

“Weren’t you afraid just now? He’d already shown the tiles. The whole room was about to praise you.”

“I was.”

“Then why stop them cheering?”

“At my father’s match, everyone in the hall said they’d understood it too.”

Yuening hugged her ledger tighter. After a few steps, she said softly, “So reading tiles begins with admitting what you haven’t read.”

“Yes.”

Yuening paused, then laughed. The laugh was brief, like sunlight escaping through a break in cloud, and she hid it almost at once.

“Then read this.” She turned her face away. “What am I thinking now?”

Qinglan looked at her.

“You are thinking that you wrote that entry yourself, and not one person believed it,” he said. “You are wondering whether your mother felt the same when she wrote, ‘This hand is suspect.’”

Yuening’s step faltered.

After a long time, she gave a quiet “Yes” and hugged the ledger closer.

“I believe it,” Qinglan said. “The cat took it. I saw him.”

Yuening did not turn, but slowly her ears grew red.

At the end of the lane, the academy’s white walls and dark roof tiles came into view. Bamboo rose above the wall, rustling in the breeze. That was the rear hill Hengchang had won last month.

This afternoon the academy game was to be reviewed; at midnight, they still had to meet that cat at the old archway. Two things, crammed into one day.

Ahead of them, Jinshi looked back, the cracked lion on his shoulder, and grinned.

“First we get the old record straight!” he shouted. “Anyone who tries sticking another needle into someone’s hand will answer to this lion head!”

(End of Chapter Four)
