Chapter 4Captain Qi

Dawn had not fully broken when someone knocked on the Rain Pavilion's door.

The knocks were not loud, but each came at a measured interval, as though the spaces between them had been marked with a ruler.

A waiter rubbed his eyes and went to open the door. As soon as it swung back, he froze.

A line of men stood in the morning mist, all in close-fitting blue-grey uniforms, blades at their waists, straight as driven nails. Their leader stepped forward, and the waiter saw his clothes clearly: deep blue-green robes embroidered front and back in silver thread with a xiezhi, clad in scales, its single horn raised, catching a cold gleam in the fog. At his waist hung a long, slender, straight blade, its ebony scabbard bound in copper, its hilt wrapped in red cord.

Xiezhi robes. A Balance blade.

Everyone in Yunchuan knew the dress of the Bureau of Balance. It was the office of the Dayu court charged with disputes under the Way of Mahjong and major cases of tile trickery.

The waiter's knees weakened. He almost fell to them.

“Qi Tingjun, Captain of the Bureau of Balance.” The man's voice was flat and even. “I am here to investigate a mahjong dispute. Is the scholar who played as a substitute here two days ago still in the building?”

◆

Qinglan had barely slept.

The White Dragon lay beneath his pillow. He turned from side to side, and every time he closed his eyes he saw the little hand on its back, five fingers spread. His father had seen it long ago. Seen it, but never managed to speak of it; he had only pressed the tile into his son's hand. Qinglan had finally drifted off near dawn, only to be woken by Jinshi's booming voice.

“On what grounds?!”

He pulled on his robe and went downstairs. Jinshi stood across the stairway like a barred door. A plaster still covered last night's wound at his temple. His dark face had turned purple.

The hall was already full. Xiao Cheng'an, Yuening and Zhumo were there. Tao Lingtao and Luo Jinke had hurried over too; white cloth bound Luo Jinke's wrist. Several Guards of Balance held the front and rear entrances. In the centre of the hall stood a young man with his hands behind his back.

Twenty-seven or twenty-eight, upright and lean, with features sharp as a drawn blade. His lips were pressed tightly together, as though he had scarcely smiled in his life.

He looked up at Qinglan. In that single glance, Qinglan felt himself examined from head to foot.

“You are Qinglan?”

“Yes.”

“From Tiandu. Left home at fourteen. Whereabouts unknown for ten years.” Qi Tingjun drew a sheet of paper from his sleeve without looking at it. “Played as a substitute at the Rain Pavilion two days ago. Ready to win in five turns, passed on a discard win, self-draw on the ninth. Overturned a twenty-eight-point deficit in one hand. A scholar who had not touched a tile for ten years.”

He put the paper away, walked to the table and opened the wooden box.

Only one snow-white Red Dragon remained inside. The severed sealing strip lay across the rim in two pieces.

“Last night, between nine and eleven, someone broke a mahjong arbitration seal and removed a piece of evidence.” He raised his eyes. “At the time, three people were in the storytelling room. The scorekeeper. The chronicler. You.”

◆

“A cat took it!” Yuening blurted out. “Catfolk! Orange-striped cat face! His name was Lu Zhaoye! I made an entry on the spot!”

She opened her ledger and pushed it towards Qi Tingjun.

He glanced at the line. “A quarter to ten at night. One catfolk male breaks seal and removes one old Red Dragon.”

“The scorekeeper's own ledger,” he said. “Written by the scorekeeper herself.”

Yuening went white.

“You were downstairs last night, troupe master.” Qi Tingjun turned to Jinshi. “Did you see anyone on the roof?”

Jinshi opened his mouth and scratched the back of his head. “I… I was inside the lion. I could only see through its mouth. I was watching the clubs down on the ground…”

A young apprentice on the stairs snorted with laughter, then hastily covered his mouth.

Yuening shot Jinshi a murderous look. He hunched his shoulders, then stiffened again and strode down from the stairs.

“So what?” he roared. “Hengchang's thugs came to steal the evidence last night, and our troupe risked our lives to stop them! My junior fellow disciple stood up for justice two days ago. He was the one who exposed the cheating! Why is the Bureau of Balance questioning him instead of hunting Hu Shan?”

Qi Tingjun did not even look at him.

“I have hunted Hu Shan for three months. From Nanchao all the way to Yunchuan. In every city, he cheats at a sworn match of honour and wins a piece of land, a shop, half a mountain. Every time, he walks away untouched.” He paused. “Only two days ago was he exposed at the table.”

“Which proves—”

“That someone understands tile trickery better than he does.” Qi Tingjun finally turned. “Do you know that Ghost Hand's finest never cheat in person? They use proxy players. The proxy takes the seat, cheats and takes the blame. The real Ghost Hand sits watching in the crowd. When the proxy is exposed, he steps forward to be the one who exposes him.”

The hall went deathly quiet.

“The day he exposes the cheat, he earns a Mahjong Jade and the whole city cheers.” Qi Tingjun spoke each word distinctly. “The very next night, evidence from ten years ago disappears under his eyes. Next time, every sworn match of honour in the city will invite him to play.”

He looked at Qinglan. “Tell me. Should I have come to see you?”

◆

Qinglan offered no defence.

“You think I am the man watching from the crowd, Captain?”

“I did not say that. But the trail ends here.”

Qi Tingjun took a wooden token from inside his robe and set it on the table. Blackened wood. A hand on the front. On the back: Nanchao.

“I have found nine of these tokens in seven cities. Every one came from a place where Ghost Hand had cheated. Last night, my men stopped a carriage outside the city, heading south. Its occupants escaped. They left this tenth token.”

Qinglan reached inside his robe and laid Pei Zhiwei's token beside it. Side by side, the two matched even in the depth of their incisions.

“I will give you the token,” Qinglan said. “I will not go with you.”

“On what grounds?”

“The rules of the Way of Mahjong.”

Biting her lip, Yuening stepped out from behind the counter. Her chin lifted again. “Article Eleven of the Mahjong Arbitration Code: suspicion of cheating in a sworn match of honour requires joint attestation by three parties—the scorekeeper, the witness and the chronicler—before the suspect may be detained for questioning. Without all three, no arrest. You do not trust my ledger. There are still the witness and the chronicler.”

Xiao Cheng'an stroked his beard and stood beside her. “I am the witness. In forty years, I have never set my seal where it did not belong.”

Zhumo squeezed through the crowd and offered a freshly copied game record in both hands. “I recorded every play of the hand two days ago, from first turn to last. You may check it tile by tile, Captain.”

Qi Tingjun took it. He read quickly and carefully. His finger paused at the sixth turn: “South passes on a discard win.” It paused again at “South wins by self-draw on Three of Dots.”

Then he rolled up the game record. “You wrote this. You could have written it wrong.”

Zhumo's ears drooped at once.

“As a chronicler, I dare not record falsely.” His voice trembled a little. “In all my life… I have kept silent only once.”

◆

Qi Tingjun did not answer.

With his hands behind his back, he paced slowly across the hall, stopped at the square wooden table and scooped a handful of tiles from the tile box beside it. They scattered across the tabletop with a clatter.

“You say you read the tiles,” he said. “Then read them. No wild tile revealed. This is only a test of your reading.”

He selected thirteen tiles and set them face down before him, hidden from everyone. Then he chose nine more, turned them over one by one and arranged them in a row in the middle of the table.

“These are my discards, from the first turn to the ninth. I am ready to win. Tell me what is in my hand, and which tile I am waiting for.”

An uproar filled the hall.

“How is that possible?” Luo Jinke burst out. “Guess thirteen concealed tiles from nine discards?”

“If you cannot,” Qi Tingjun said evenly, “you come with me.”

Jinshi's knuckles cracked as his fists tightened. Yuening clutched her ledger until her knuckles went white.

Qinglan approached the table and looked down at the nine tiles.

North Wind. West Wind. Nine of Characters. One of Dots. Eight of Bamboo. Two of Characters. Five of Dots. Seven of Characters. Six of Characters.

He studied them for a long time. Long enough that when the old sugar seller swallowed at the doorway, everyone heard it.

Qi Tingjun did not hurry him. He stood beside the table with folded arms, watching Qinglan's hands rather than the tiles. Those hands hung motionless at his sides. Not even a fingertip trembled.

Qi Tingjun's eyelid gave the faintest twitch.

“North Wind on the first turn. West Wind on the second. You clear the honour tiles first; both Winds were isolated tiles in your hand.” Qinglan began. “Nine of Characters on the third turn, One of Dots on the fourth. You discard terminals; your hand is moving towards the middle numbers. Eight of Bamboo on the fifth. To discard a simple tile so early suggests that your Bamboo tiles already form a group and the Eight does not connect. Two of Characters on the sixth. On the eighth and ninth, Seven of Characters and Six of Characters.”

“And the seventh?” Qi Tingjun asked.

Qinglan raised his head.

“This Five of Dots on the seventh turn,” he said. “You never discarded it.”

◆

Everyone in the hall froze.

“You laid the nine tiles down in one continuous movement,” Qinglan said. “Your hand never stopped. Except here. You put this tile down, then shifted it half an inch to the right. A genuine discard takes no thought to place. A false one does. You have to decide which turn it belongs in.”

Qi Tingjun's face showed nothing.

“Hands alone can mislead,” Qinglan continued. “So let us work it out. If this tile were genuine, the plays around it would not fit.”

His finger hovered over the tabletop. “On the eighth and ninth turns, you broke up Seven of Characters and Six of Characters. You had too many Characters and needed to dismantle a group. Afterwards, one sequence remained: Three, Four and Five of Characters. You cleared North Wind and West Wind early, but not a single East Wind is visible. And when you chose your tiles, you reached first for two White Dragons. Those give you a triplet and a pair of eyes. Your Bamboo group is Five, Six and Seven of Bamboo, which is why the Eight did not connect.”

“Three, Four and Five of Characters. Five, Six and Seven of Bamboo. Three East Winds. Two White Dragons,” Qinglan said. “Eleven tiles. The other two are Dots.”

“Which two?”

“Four of Dots. Six of Dots.”

He met Qi Tingjun's eyes. “A man holding Four of Dots and Six of Dots does not discard Five of Dots on his seventh turn. It is the tile he needs most. So you inserted this Five of Dots afterwards.”

“You put the tile you wanted into your own discard pool,” Qinglan said softly. “To make anyone watching think the safest tile was the very one you needed.”

“You are waiting for Five of Dots, Captain.”

A stir ran through the crowd. Someone whispered, “A planted discard? But the Five of Dots is right there, isn't it?”

Xiao Cheng'an closed his folding fan and lowered his voice, as he did at a crucial moment in a story. “Everyone, a player looks in the discard pool of the player opposite for a ‘safe tile’. If that player has already discarded it, you follow suit, and people tend to think that is safer. But suppose someone puts the very tile he needs into his discard pool first—”

“Then he waits for somebody to hand it over without a worry.” The old sugar seller drew a sharp breath. “That's fouler than a poisoned needle!”

◆

A pin could have been heard dropping in the hall.

Qi Tingjun stared at Qinglan for a long time. Then he turned over the thirteen tiles before him, one by one.

Three of Characters. Four of Characters. Five of Characters. Five of Bamboo. Six of Bamboo. Seven of Bamboo. East Wind. East Wind. East Wind. White Dragon. White Dragon. Four of Dots. Six of Dots.

Not one wrong.

Cries broke from the crowd. The old sugar seller slapped his thigh in excitement. “A miracle! A miracle!” Jinshi's jaw hung open and would not close. Yuening let out a long breath, only then realising she had been holding it.

Zhumo hugged the scroll, his eyes reddening. “Qing Yuanshan's reading… Exactly the same…”

“I learned this trick,” Qi Tingjun said at last, “from Ghost Hand. Three months ago, in Nanchao, someone planted a discard just like this. An old player was tricked into breaking up a hand he treasured more than life. Half the city's dockyards were lost.”

He looked at the Five of Dots on the table. “Not one person there saw through it.”

Slowly, he unfastened the Balance blade from his waist and laid it on the table.

The hand that set down the blade was shaking.

“Ten years ago. That match at Wangjiang Teahouse,” he said. “I was there too.”

Everyone stared.

The scroll in Zhumo's hands slipped half open with a rustle. He gazed wide-eyed at Qi Tingjun's face, as though turning the pages of memory. “You were… the one standing behind the match supervisor that day…”

He swallowed the rest.

Qinglan's fingers tightened suddenly.

There had been hundreds of people in Wangjiang Teahouse that day. He remembered only his father's straight back, the North-seat player's hand opening the wall, the broken tiles on the floor and the blood in his own palms. He remembered no boy in xiezhi robes.

“You were there?” His voice was rough. “What did you see?”

Qi Tingjun did not answer. He fastened the blade to his waist again, and his face grew cold once more, as though that crack had never opened.

“I believe you can read the tiles,” he said. “I do not yet believe you.”

◆

“The Way of Mahjong requires joint attestation by three parties. I will not arrest you.” Qi Tingjun turned to Yuening. “Do you know the academy in the north of the city?”

Yuening stared. “Hu Shan won its rear hill in a sworn match of honour last month.”

“Master Wen and Assistant Tutor Du still refuse to accept it. They insist something was wrong with that hand, but they have no proof. I have asked them to hold a new match at the academy this afternoon. Every discard will be recorded in public. I will supervise in person.” He looked at Qinglan. “You will play. If you truly are like your father, convince the people of the academy. If you belong to Ghost Hand—”

He said no more, only rested a hand lightly on his hilt.

“If I win,” Qinglan said, “you tell me what you saw ten years ago.”

Qi Tingjun gave him a look.

“Win first.”

He left with his men.

◆

Jinshi dropped onto a bench. It creaked and nearly came apart.

“These people from the Bureau of Balance,” he said angrily, “are worse to deal with than Hengchang's thugs!”

“He is not a bad man,” Qinglan said.

“How do you know?”

“His hand shook when he set down his blade.”

Luo Jinke came over, cradling her bandaged wrist. “Young Master Qing, I'm coming to the academy this afternoon too.”

“Your hand—”

“My hand cannot play. My eyes can still see,” Luo Jinke said. “I want to see for myself how that needle reached me two days ago. Who handed over the tea tray.”

Behind her, Tao Lingtao gave a small nod.

Watching Qinglan, Yuening suddenly felt that this scholar was harder to understand than she had thought.

Zhumo pressed a freshly copied game record into Qinglan's hand. “The academy's match last month. I went there last night to copy it.”

Qinglan read down the lines. Halfway through, his brow furrowed.

“Yuening. Look.”

She leaned close and slid a finger down the record. Her face changed. “Third turn. South discards Two of Dots. West calls pung, exposing three. Seventh turn, East discards another. Eleventh turn, North discards one more… Five Two of Dots tiles. Again.”

Exactly like the hand ten years ago.

◆

They were about to leave when the door curtain lifted and a woman entered.

She was in her early thirties, wearing a deep-blue gown, a black side braid falling over her shoulder. In her hand was an elmwood medicine chest. She looked at no one's face when she entered. Her eyes went straight to the white cloth around Luo Jinke's wrist.

“Lan Ruoheng. I practise at the apothecary in the east of the city. Proprietor Tao sent for me last night. She said a dyer had been pricked with a needle, and the swelling had not gone down in two days.”

She unwrapped the cloth. Luo Jinke's wrist was swollen like a steamed bun; the blue-purple ring around the puncture had spread to an area half the size of a palm. Lan Ruoheng touched a silver needle to the skin beside the wound and lifted a drop of dark-red blood. She held it near her nose and smelled it.

“The needle was coated in bone-numbing herb sap. Not fatal, but contact makes the fingers stiff for three to five days. You cannot hold things securely. If treatment is delayed, a lingering tremor can last for over a month.” She sprinkled powder over the wound. “It is not local. In all the land of Tianyuan, only the sea-goods shops of Nanchao sell it.”

Nanchao again.

Qinglan and Yuening exchanged a look.

Lan Ruoheng bound the wound, wrote a prescription and told Luo Jinke to stay away from the dye vats for seven days. As she left, she looked back. “I heard that after the academy lost its rear hill, Assistant Tutor Du fell badly ill. His hands shook so much he could not hold a brush. At the time, I thought distress had overwhelmed him. Now I wonder if he too was pricked with a needle, and the delay in treatment left him with a lingering tremor.”

◆

On the way to the academy, Jinshi led the procession, the cracked lion head on his shoulder. He said he would find the academy's carpenter and have it repaired. Zhumo walked along leafing through the old record, murmuring to himself.

Yuening walked beside Qinglan with the ledger in her arms. After a while, she spoke suddenly.

“That Five of Dots. Did you really work it out by watching his hands?”

“Yes.”

“And the White Dragons? You saw them when he chose his tiles?”

“Yes.”

Yuening glared at him. “And you call that reading the tiles. You were peeking.”

“Reading the tiles…” Qinglan considered. “It has never meant reading only the tiles.”

Yuening paused, then laughed. The laugh was brief, like sunlight escaping through a break in cloud, and she hid it almost at once.

“Then read this.” She turned her face away. “What am I thinking now?”

Qinglan looked at her.

“You are thinking that you wrote that entry yourself, and not one person believed it,” he said. “You are wondering whether your mother felt the same when she wrote, ‘This hand is suspect.’”

Yuening's step faltered.

After a long time, she gave a quiet “Yes” and hugged the ledger closer.

“I believe it,” Qinglan said. “The cat took it. I saw him.”

Yuening did not turn, but slowly her ears grew red.

At the end of the lane, the academy's white walls and dark roof tiles came into view. Bamboo rose above the wall, rustling in the breeze. That was the rear hill Hengchang had won last month.

Six hours remained until the afternoon appointment with Qi Tingjun. Sixteen until Lu Zhaoye's midnight meeting.

Ahead of them, Jinshi looked back, the cracked lion on his shoulder, and grinned.

“What is there to be afraid of? This afternoon, at the academy, we'll drag that extra Two of Dots into the light, right under the Bureau of Balance's nose!”

(End of Chapter Four)
