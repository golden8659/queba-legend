About the story

In a world governed by the Way of Mahjong, disputes are settled at the table of justice. A sworn match of honour takes the place of swords: three parties bear witness, the game record is open to all, and victory decides what is just.

Ten years ago, before the Tianyuan Mahjong Assembly, mahjong master Qing Yuanshan was disgraced at a sworn match of honour when an extra Red Dragon was pulled from his wall. Overnight, the cleanest hands in the land became the dirtiest. After his death, his son Qinglan kept the one tile his father had left him, a blank White Dragon, hidden for ten years—and swore never to touch a tile again.

But fate brings him to Yunchuan. Beside the Three-Coloured River, the girls of a dye workshop must win a single match to keep their waterway, and someone at the table is cheating. Qinglan takes a seat as a substitute player. What he exposes is more than a trick: it is Ghost Hand, the shadow that has watched him for ten years.

An unlikely band gathers around him: Yuening, the scorekeeper whose abacus never leaves her hand, who trusts the ledger over any personal tie; Jinshi, the lion-dance master with a voice louder than the roar of his lion head and a heart warmer than anyone’s; and Zhumo, the chronicler who records every game, yet has kept silent about one for a decade.

Legend says the God of Mahjong once scattered 136 Heavenly Tiles across the mortal world. They never help anyone win, but they remember every game they have touched. What does the White Dragon in Qinglan’s hand remember?

A man who swore never to touch a tile must play an honest hand to clear his father’s name.
